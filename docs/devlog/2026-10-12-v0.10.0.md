# v0.10.0: A place for your photos

*2026-10-12, Paul Carouge*

Until now, a photo in Alcove was just a file with a nice thumbnail. This release starts phase 2: every
picture and video you put anywhere in Files now also shows up in **Photos**, a timeline sorted by the
date it was taken, month by month.

## What it does

The grid uses justified rows, like the photo apps you know: each row keeps the pictures' real shapes
and fills the width exactly, so portraits and panoramas sit side by side without cropping. Click one to
open a dark, full-screen viewer with the photo or the video, arrow keys to move along the timeline, and a
details panel: when, which camera and lens, the exposure, and where (with a link to OpenStreetMap; no
map tiles are loaded by Alcove itself). A heart marks favorites, and two filters show only favorites or
only videos. Everything updates live: upload from Files in one tab, watch the photos arrive in another.

## How it works

You don't import anything: the server notices new images and videos and processes them in the background.
It reads EXIF for photos and asks `ffprobe` about videos and HEIC files (iPhones), then `ffmpeg` renders a
small thumbnail and a larger preview. Your originals are never touched. Only two files are processed at a
time so that a Raspberry Pi stays usable while it digests a few thousand photos, and work interrupted by
a restart picks up where it stopped.

That is why ffmpeg is now part of the Docker image (a move from distroless to Alpine, still running as
a non-root user) and offered by the native installer. Without it, ordinary JPEG, PNG and WebP photos
still work; videos and HEIC wait for it.

## What went wrong

- **Square photos everywhere.** The first screenshots showed a perfectly regular grid of squares, which
  is not what justified rows should look like. The test images had EXIF data claiming 8×8 pixels while
  being 1200×800. Silly test data, but real cameras and editors do the same: crop a photo and many tools
  keep the old EXIF size. The server now trusts the image itself and only uses EXIF for formats it can't
  decode.
- **A timeline that loaded forever.** In Svelte 5, an effect re-runs when any state it reads changes, and
  the first page load read the "loading" flag it then set. One `untrack` later, it loads once.
- **Videos out of order.** A video gets its real date only after `ffprobe` has looked at it, so it first
  appeared at the top, dated "now", and stayed there when its date was corrected. Live updates now
  re-sort the timeline.
