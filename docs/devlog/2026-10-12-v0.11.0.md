# v0.11.0: Albums, and the people you share them with

*2026-10-12, Paul Carouge*

A timeline is good for scrolling through your life; it is bad at "the summer in Provence". This release
adds albums, and the part that makes them worth having on a family server: sharing them.

## What it does

Albums are made from the timeline. A new **Select** button turns every photo into a checkbox; pick a few,
press **Add to album**, choose an album or type a new name. The viewer can do the same for a single photo.
An album page shows its photos, lets you pick a cover, rename it, and remove what does not belong.

**Share** opens a small dialog: type the email of someone who has an account on your server, and decide
whether they *can view* or *can add photos*. Contributors add their own pictures from their own timeline;
they never get access to anything else you own. Remove someone and their access is gone at once, along
with the photos they had added. They can also leave by themselves.

## How it is built

This was the first time a record in Alcove could be read by someone other than its owner, so I wrote down
the rules before writing code ([ADR 0018](../adr/0018-albums-and-sharing-between-accounts.md)):

- your **drive stays private**, always. Nothing in this release widens access to files;
- a photo becomes readable by the people of an album it is in, through one database rule, and the
  timeline query still only lists your own photos;
- the original of a shared video or photo is served by a dedicated route that asks that same rule.

That last point had a consequence. Until now, ordinary JPEG photos used PocketBase's built-in thumbnails,
made from the drive file itself, which participants must not touch. So every photo now gets its own
thumbnail and preview, made by ffmpeg, or by a small pure-Go thumbnailer when ffmpeg is missing (it even
knows how to turn sideways phone pictures upright). Existing libraries are processed once more at the next
start.

The tests that matter most are the boring ones: a stranger sees nothing, a viewer cannot add, a
contributor cannot add someone else's photo nor remove yours, and a removed member loses access
immediately. They passed the first time, which is the nice side of expressing access as rules rather
than scattered `if`s.

## What went wrong

- **A crash only the tests could see.** The thumbnail backfill made the photo worker's start-up a little
  longer, long enough for it to touch the database after a test had already closed it. Shutting down is
  now orderly: start-up scans are tracked, and nothing is queued once the server is stopping.
- **A button under a panel.** The end-to-end test hung on "Add to album": the upload panel, which stays in
  the bottom corner after uploading, covered the selection bar. A real person would have hit the same
  wall, so the selection bar moved to the top of the page.
- **"Shared by someone".** Accounts without a display name showed up as "someone" on shared albums. The
  owner's email is used instead.
- **A one-pixel test.** A check that a rotated photo kept red on top failed on a 2×1 image: JPEG stores
  colour at half resolution, so red and blue blended into purple. The test image is bigger now.
