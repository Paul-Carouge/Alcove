# v0.12.0: Showing the photos, not where they were taken

*2026-10-12, Paul Carouge*

Albums could already be shared with people who have an account on your server. Most of the people you
want to show holiday pictures to do not, so albums now get public links, like files: a URL, optionally a
password and an expiry date, revocable at any time from the Shared page.

## Privacy was the real work

A photo is more than its pixels. Phones write the exact GPS position into every picture, along with the
camera model and sometimes its serial number. Sending that to anyone who has a link would be careless,
so public album links:

- list photos without any location, camera or lens information;
- serve photos only as the previews and thumbnails Alcove generates, which are re-encoded and carry no
  metadata at all (a test checks there is no EXIF block in what visitors receive);
- never serve the original photo files.

Videos are the exception: there is no transcoding yet, so they play from the original file, and a video
can contain a location too. The share dialog says so plainly. Stripped video transcodes will close that
gap later.

The link shows the album as it is now: photos you add appear, photos you remove or delete disappear, and
deleting the album kills the link.

## Reusing what existed

File links already had everything that matters: a 256-bit token stored only as a hash, bcrypt passwords,
short-lived unlock tokens, rate limits on password attempts. A link now simply points to a file, a folder
or an album. The visitor page reuses the photo grid and viewer from the app, with downloads turned off.

## What went wrong

- The first album link pointed at nothing. When the server created the link, it remembered that the
  target was an album but not *which* album. Every test that opened a link got "not found", which is at
  least the safe way to be wrong.
- The end-to-end test could not type the password: "Password" also matched the "Protect with a password"
  checkbox. The test asks for the exact label now.
