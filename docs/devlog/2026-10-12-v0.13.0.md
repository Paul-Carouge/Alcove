# v0.13.0: Twice the same photo

*2026-10-12, Paul Carouge*

Every photo library collects copies: the picture you sent yourself on a messaging app (now smaller and
recompressed), the same folder imported twice, eight shots of the same sunset. This release finds them,
and adds filters to the timeline.

## Duplicates

**Photos → Duplicates** shows groups: identical copies, and photos that *look* the same. In each group
Alcove suggests the copy to keep (highest resolution, then largest file, then the oldest) and marks the
others; click a picture to change your mind, then move the rest to the trash, one group at a time or all
at once. Nothing is ever deleted without you, and the trash gives everything back. Bursts you want to keep
can be marked "not duplicates"; the group comes back only if another similar photo joins it.

Identical files are easy: they share a SHA-256. "Looks the same" needs a fingerprint. Each photo gets a
64-bit *difference hash* of its thumbnail: shrink it to 9×8 grey pixels and note, for each pixel, whether
its right neighbour is brighter. A resized or recompressed copy changes only a few of those bits. Finding
pairs within 6 bits among tens of thousands of photos without comparing everything with everything uses
an old trick: split the 64 bits into 7 parts; two fingerprints that close must share at least one part
exactly, so only those are compared. A test checks this against brute force on random fingerprints.

## What went wrong, and made it better

The first try grouped everything in my test library with everything else. My test pictures are smooth
gradients, and for a gradient every pixel is darker than the next: the fingerprint is 64 ones, for all of
them. Real photos are rarely that smooth, but skies, blank pages and dark shots come close. Such
low-information fingerprints are now ignored for "looks the same" (identical files are still found).

The screenshots then showed a green picture grouped with a pink one: same layout, different colours, and
the fingerprint only looks at brightness. It now also carries the average colour, and similar photos must
have close colours and the same proportions (a crop or a rotation is not a copy).

The new filters had a bug of their own: change the year while the timeline is still loading, and the
answer to the *previous* question arrived last and won. Each view now has a number, and late answers for
an old view are dropped.

## Filters

A **Filters** button narrows the timeline by year, camera and "with a location", using only the years and
cameras actually present in your library, with counts.
