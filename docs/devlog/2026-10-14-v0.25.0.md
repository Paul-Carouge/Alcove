# v0.25.0: The little things

*2026-10-14, Paul Carouge*

The brief for this release was "the practical things premium photo apps have". None of them is big; all
of them are noticed when missing.

## Looking at photos

The web viewer now behaves like a photo viewer rather than a picture in a dialog. The wheel zooms around
the cursor, a double click zooms to the spot, two fingers pinch on a touch screen, and a zoomed photo can
be dragged around. Swiping changes photo on phones and tablets. Space starts a slideshow, `f` toggles the
favorite, `Delete` moves to the trash and the next photo shows. The two neighbours of the current photo
are loaded in the background, so the arrow keys feel instant even on a slow server.

In the app, a double tap zooms where you tapped, dragging a photo down closes the viewer (as in every
gallery app), and a photo or video can be saved back to the phone's gallery.

## Finding and choosing

Memories bring back what was taken on this day in previous years, as cards above the timeline. The phone
sends its own date, so "today" follows its time zone rather than the server's. On the web, Shift extends a
selection like a file manager, a month can be selected in one click, and a small year strip on the side
jumps through a long timeline. In the app, pinching the grid makes photos bigger or smaller; it listens to
raw pointer events so scrolling is never disturbed.

## Taking things home

A selection of photos, a folder or a whole album can be downloaded as one ZIP. The server checks access
and resolves the files first, then hands out a ten-minute link the browser downloads like any file, so
nothing has to be held in memory. Photos and videos are stored without recompression, two files with the
same name become "name (2)", and a folder keeps its tree.

## What went wrong

- Even folded, the upload panel still covered the last rows of a list: a test could not reach a folder's
  menu behind it. The page now reserves the panel's height at the bottom, so everything can be scrolled
  above it.
- A test meant to Shift-click the third photo clicked "Select the month" instead, whose name also begins
  with "Select". Labels matter to tests as much as to screen readers.
- The OpenAPI generator turned a list of free-form objects into Dart that does not compile. Normal tests
  did not notice because they do not import the generated client; the screenshot tests did. The schema now
  names the item type, and the next release adds a compile check of the generated client to CI.
