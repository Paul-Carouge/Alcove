# v0.28.0: Lin

*2026-10-14, Paul Carouge*

A complete redesign, on every platform. The brief: modern, calm, intuitive, premium and clean. Of three
directions, I chose "Lin": the warmth of paper, near-black ink, a deep sage green, and a serif for headings.
The feeling should be closer to a good notebook than to a dashboard.

## One palette, two platforms

Everything starts in the design tokens, the single file both the web and the app read their colours from.
The new palette went in there first, light and dark, and passed the contrast test that checks every
text-and-background pair against WCAG AA. Corners grew (8 to 24 px), shadows became faint and warm, and two
font families were added: Inter for reading, Fraunces for titles, with its optical sizes, so large titles
get finer details than small ones. Both fonts ship with Alcove; nothing is fetched from a font service.

## The web

The shell got quieter: the sidebar sits on the paper and the current section rises as a small white sheet.
Titles are set in Fraunces with an item count under them. Empty pages share one design, an icon inside a
small arch, the shape of the logo. Albums look like little stacks of prints. On phones, dialogs slide up
as bottom sheets with full-width buttons. The sign-in page now opens with a large serif line, "A calm home
for your files."

The pass also found older bugs: on phones, Photos and Documents were slightly wider than the screen, so the
browser zoomed out and the page scrolled sideways, and the viewer's close button could be pushed off
screen.

## The app: Material 3 Expressive

The app follows Material 3 Expressive, the newest version of Google's design system, so it feels at home
on a recent Android phone. Flutter 3.44 does not ship its components yet, so what Flutter has is used
through the theme (buttons that morph when pressed, the new progress indicators), and the rest is built
with plain Flutter, without a new dependency: the expressive shapes, a loading indicator that morphs between
them, a floating toolbar in the photo viewer, a "+" menu that unfolds from its button, large titles that
shrink as you scroll, and spring motion everywhere, turned off when the phone asks for reduced motion.

## What went wrong

- The colour scheme of Material 3 is usually derived from one seed colour. Here it is built by hand from the
  tokens, otherwise the app's greens would drift from the web's.
- Flutter tests draw shadows as solid black and scrims darker than they are. Every screenshot had to be
  read with that in mind; the real check of the motion is on a phone.
- One end-to-end test failed even before the redesign: it typed in the search box before a page change had
  finished, and the page reset the focus. It now waits for the page.

The two redesigns were done in parallel by design agents working from the same brief, then checked
together: lint, unit, widget and end-to-end tests, the APK build, and screenshots of every screen.
