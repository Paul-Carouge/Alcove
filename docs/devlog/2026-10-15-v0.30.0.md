# v0.30.0: Calendar, and a lighter app

*2026-10-15, Paul Carouge*

Phase 5 is complete: after notes, the calendar. And the Android app lost two thirds of its weight.

## A calendar shaped like iCalendar

The calendar lives inside Alcove for now; sync with the phone's own calendar apps (CalDAV) comes later. To
make that later step easy, the data follows iCalendar from the start: times in UTC, a stable `uid` per
event, repeat rules written as RFC 5545 `RRULE` strings, and all-day events stored as whole dates with an
exclusive end, like iCalendar's `DTEND`. ADR 0028 lists what CalDAV will still need: a time zone per event,
exceptions to a series, and app passwords for clients that cannot do two-factor sign-in.

Repeats are expanded on each device for the dates on screen, by a small function written for Alcove (no
date library was installed, and none was needed), in TypeScript for the web and Dart for the app. Both
ports skip the dates that do not exist, such as a monthly event on the 31st in a 30-day month, as the RFC
says.

## What went wrong

Time is hard, and daylight saving time is where it shows. The review found three bugs in the app that
only appeared on the days the clocks change: a series ending on 29 March in Paris got one occurrence too
many, moving an all-day event across that day made it a day shorter, and new events started at 10:00
instead of 9:00. All three came from the same mistake: adding a number of hours to a local midnight, when
a day is not always 24 hours long. The tests had run under Honolulu and Tokyo, two zones without daylight
saving time, so they missed it. They now also run under Paris and New York.

Two devices in two languages could also each create a first calendar ("Personal" and "Personnel"). A
`is_default` flag with a unique index now lets only one exist.

## A third of the size

The APK held the engine and the app three times, once per processor type: 62 MB. Releases now also ship one
APK per processor type, and the in-app updater asks Android which one the phone needs: 21.5 MB for a recent
phone. The universal APK stays, so apps up to 0.29 still update.

The review caught one trap: Flutter numbers split APKs differently (2031 instead of 31), so a phone updated
with the arm64 APK would have refused the next universal one as a downgrade. The build now gives all APKs
the same number and fails if they differ.

## Sturdier

The app now reuses one connection instead of opening a new one per request (18 ms instead of 92 ms per
request in a test over HTTPS), opens without waiting for a slow server, and gives up on a silent server
after 30 seconds. Making startup faster opened a race of its own: a slow sign-in refresh answering after
you signed out could sign you back in. Every session change now has a number, and an answer meant for an
older one is ignored.

Last, a fix from real use: typing `192.168.68.51:8090` in the app tried https first and failed with a
certificate error. The app now falls back to http for home-network addresses.
