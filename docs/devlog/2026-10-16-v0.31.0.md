# v0.31.0: The app, reworked from real use

*2026-10-16, Paul Carouge*

This release comes from using the app on a real phone. Four remarks: the bottom bar was too full, there
was a large empty band above every page title, PDFs left the app for the browser, and a few everyday
comforts were missing.

## A floating bar with five places

Seven destinations did not fit Material's guidance of five at most. Albums moved inside Photos (a
"Library | Albums" switch), the account moved to an avatar at the top right, and the rest became a floating
pill: Photos, Files, Documents, Notes, Calendar. Only the selected item shows its label. The bar hides
when you scroll down, comes back when you scroll up, and stays put when the system asks for reduced
motion. Tabs are now built on first visit, so the app makes one network call at startup instead of four.

The header shrank from 132 dp plus an empty line to 72 dp: the serif title sits on the same line as the
actions, right under the status bar.

## Reading files without leaving the app

Android has had a PDF renderer built in since version 5. Using it through a small native bridge adds
nothing to the app's size, where a bundled engine would have added 4 to 5 MB per processor type. Only the
pages on screen are rendered, at the width they are shown, sharper as you zoom. Text, code, JSON and CSV
open as text. Every viewer can share, open with another app, or save to Downloads.

## What went wrong

The security audit found that "Share to Alcove" trusted whatever another app sent: a malicious app could
have pointed it at Alcove's own private files and, once you tapped Send, had them uploaded to your drive.
Only `content://` links from other apps are accepted now, files inside Alcove's own storage are refused,
copies are size-limited, and names are cleaned of right-to-left tricks that make `fdp.apk` look like a PDF.

The code review found the rest: a zoomed scanned PDF could run the phone out of memory and crash the app,
two large pages could evict each other from the cache forever, the bar could stay hidden after opening a
short folder, and a second copy of the app could start when sharing from some apps (a small dedicated
share screen now hands the files to the running app). Uploads queued by one account no longer go out
under the next account that signs in.

None of this ran on a real device yet; the next round of feedback will tell.
